Annual tax calculation — England, 2026/27 rules

Existing crystallised withdrawals have no attached tax-free cash. Both crystallised accounts are treated as fully taxable; tax allowances are applied to your total income. Pension withdrawals can be covered by your personal allowance, but other income and your State Pension use the same allowance. Unit Trust gains and income are split by ownership. Each person has a separate tax calculation.

Ownership defaults to 100% yours; change it for a joint holding. Your partner’s private pension increases by 2.5% by default, and tax thresholds are held constant unless you change that assumption. These are assumptions to check, not predictions of future tax policy. Distribution yield is included within the total investment return and assumed reinvested; reinvested income increases the Unit Trust cost basis. Cash savings interest is capitalised and taxed separately; ISA interest is exempt.

This is a full-year projection from the balances above, not a reconciliation of the partly completed 2026/27 tax year. Do not subtract past withdrawals from these current balances again. Historical employment income, pension payments and gains before the forecast start are not included. No other gains/losses, reliefs, Marriage Allowance or external investment income are assumed. Both people's pensions continue throughout; survivor changes are not modelled.

Sources checked 22 October 2026: Income Tax · Capital Gains Tax · Dividends · Savings · Pension allowance. Rates are held constant in the forecast. Personal allowances are allocated to ordinary income, then savings, then dividends; unusual income mixes may need a different allocation.

Forecast assumptions

Mid-year timing applies half the annual growth before withdrawals and the remainder afterwards. This is an annual approximation, not monthly modelling. All crystallised pension withdrawals share your personal allowance with your other taxable income. Under phased drawdown, up to 25% of each new tranche moves to cash tax-free. Under UFPLS, up to 25% of each direct payment is tax-free. Both are capped by the remaining lump sum allowance you enter; previous tax-free cash across all pensions counts towards that allowance.
